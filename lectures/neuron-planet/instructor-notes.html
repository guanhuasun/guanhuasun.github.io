Numerical conventions

V in mV, time in ms, current in nA, capacitance in nF, and conductance in μS. Default Cₘ=0.1 nF, gₗ=0.01 μS, Eₗ=−65 mV, τ=10 ms. Applied current is inward positive and leak is outward positive. Passive labs 1–4 use exact piecewise exponential solutions. Lab 5 compares Euler with exact unforced decay. The LIF lab uses analytical threshold crossings, immediate reset, and separate spike-event marks; it does not generate a physiological action-potential waveform.

31 / 56 · From circuit to equation · 1.5 min

Applied current splits between two branches

Explain signs precisely: I_app is inward positive, while I_C and I_L are branch currents from the inside node toward the outside. Capacitive current is the circuit representation of changing surface charge, not ions crossing the insulating lipid interior. The plotted exact RC step begins at V=E_L; I_C=I_app exp(−t/τ) and I_L=I_app[1−exp(−t/τ)]. Their sum is constant. At steady state I_C=0. At pulse offset the applied current becomes zero but the branch currents can remain nonzero and cancel.

Lab 5 · Check the numerical timestep

This diagnostic uses no input pulse and V(0)=−55 mV, so pulse-edge sampling cannot confound numerical accuracy. Exact decay is the reference. Error evolution for Euler is multiplied by 1−Δt/τ per full step. Between τ and 2τ, the numerical deviation alternates sign while shrinking; at 2τ it does not decay; above 2τ it grows. Δt=τ reaches E_L in one numerical step, which is stable but inaccurate. A final partial step ends at exactly 100 ms. The axes autoscale if unstable values require it, and the interface states that clearly.

Leaky integrate-and-fire adds a threshold rule

Connect the new model directly to the equation students already simulated. It is the leaky integrate-and-fire model, abbreviated LIF. Explain that a threshold crossing records an event and an immediate reset changes the state. The reset voltage is below threshold. The example has no refractory period. This abstraction is useful for spike timing, while detailed active membrane currents remain for Hodgkin–Huxley in the following lecture.

Live LIF model · From no firing to repeated firing

Start with I=0.2 nA and threshold −50 mV, then lower the current to 0.1 nA to show no firing. Increase current and compare the spacing of events. A threshold closer to reset generally requires less charging per event. When the constant-input equilibrium is at or below threshold, an initially lower voltage never reaches threshold in finite time. A brief pulse may also end before a threshold crossing. This lab finds crossing times analytically; plotting resolution does not determine spike times. The downloadable Python example remains the passive membrane exercise.

54 / 56 · Where the next lecture begins · 0.75 min

What Hodgkin–Huxley adds

Contrast the modeling goals. LIF retains passive integration and adds a prescribed threshold/reset event rule. Hodgkin–Huxley instead explains the action-potential time course through voltage-dependent ionic currents and evolving channel states. Current conservation and membrane capacitance remain the same framework. Keep the m,h,n equations and sodium/potassium kinetics for the next lectures. The displayed spike is schematic, not a Hodgkin–Huxley simulation.
